What this screens for

Something that has fallen a long way, has stopped behaving like a downtrend, and is coiled tightly enough that the distance to invalidation is small. Damage on its own generates value traps. Exhaustion on its own is noise. The tradeable object is the conjunction, which is why the output should usually be short.

1Depth

Log drawdown from the highest close over the look-back window, defaulting to five years and 35%. This is a value proxy: long-horizon reversal in the De Bondt and Thaler sense, measured over three to five years rather than the twelve months where cross-sectional momentum lives. The highest 60-day mean option exists for the parabola problem: an asset that doubles and halves is mechanically 50% off its high while sitting exactly where it started, because a single print set the reference. Using a 60-day mean as the reference leaves a sustained top almost unchanged and strips most of the height out of a three-week spike.

2Duration

Months since that high, default twelve. Cheap, and it does more work than it looks like: it removes the entire population of fast crashes that have not finished crashing, and the freshly unwound parabolas along with them. The optional upper bound excludes assets whose peak is so old that the decline is better described as secular.

3No new lows

Trading days since the last running 252-day low, bounded on both sides. The lower bound, default 126, is the definitional condition — the thing that separates this from a falling knife. The upper bound, default 504, requires that a 252-day low actually happened within the past two years, so an asset that has drifted quietly sideways for a decade without ever being punished does not qualify. This gate is the mechanism that resolves the conflict between intermediate-horizon momentum, which says losers keep losing, and long-horizon reversal, which says they eventually stop: it is a direct test of whether the momentum has decayed yet.

4Curvature

Default is the slope of the 100-day moving average over the trailing 60 sessions, combined with a requirement that price sits above that average. The slope alone has a known artifact — the average moves by the new bar minus the dropped bar, so a large down day rolling out of the window can turn the slope positive with no new information in current price at all. Requiring price above the average cannot be produced by old data leaving the sample, which is what closes the hole. The quadratic alternative fits a parabola to log price over the trailing window and asks that it be bowl-shaped and rising at the right edge; it measures the second derivative directly rather than through a proxy, and has no base effect by construction.

This gate is the slowest to confirm, and it will often fire months after gate 5. That is the case for running the screen with gate 4 switched off as a watchlist, and switching it on as the trigger — the Watchlist preset does exactly that.

5Volatility contraction

Realised volatility over the trailing 20 sessions, percentile-ranked against its own five-year distribution, required to sit below the 30th percentile now and to have exceeded the 70th at some point in the prior 18 months. The two-part structure matters. Compression on its own picks up assets that have simply always been quiet; compression following expansion is what a base actually looks like, and it is the condition that makes the eventual move violent. Volatility is computed from closes rather than true range because the ratios have no meaningful high and low, and consistency across the two screens is worth more than the small gain from intraday data.

6Asymmetry, which is not a gate

The gates decide whether the setup exists. Whether you want it is a separate question, and a continuous one. Each row reports the distance from here down to the base low — the level whose violation falsifies the thesis, handed to you by gate 3 — and the distance up to the prior high, and the ratio between them. A name that cleared depth by 60% but sits far above its invalidation level is a worse holding than one that barely cleared at 36% and sits just above it. Sort on payoff, not on how deeply anything satisfied gate 1.

7What the funnel is for

Five gates that each pass roughly a third of the universe would leave under one percent if they were independent. They are not independent — deep drawdowns are where volatility contraction happens — so the real number is higher, but the point stands: instrument the attrition. If one gate eliminates almost everything and the rest eliminate nothing, the screen is that one gate wearing a costume. If the final bar reads three, the honest reading is that the setup is absent, not that you have found a shortlist of three.

8What was removed from the source list

Fifteen tickers were dropped. Three are structurally broken for this purpose; the rest are duplicates that would have produced two events for one observation. Add any of them back through the Universe editor if you disagree.

9Known limits

This is a live screen, not a backtest, and it is not investment advice. Three things it does not do. It cannot tell you the base rate — of all the assets that clear a deep drawdown plus six months without a new low, some meaningful fraction go on to make a new low anyway, and only an event study over decades will tell you what that fraction is. It has no non-price anchor, so an asset in genuine terminal decline can clear every gate; gate 3 absorbs more of that than you would expect, since things going to zero keep making new lows, but the slow-obsolescence case survives. And events cluster in time — one bad October will fire forty of these at once, which is one observation and not forty.
