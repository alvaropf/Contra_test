How the cascade works

Five gates, evaluated in order. A candidate has to clear each one independently — there is no score, and nothing compensates for anything else. Depth without exhaustion is a falling knife; exhaustion without depth is an asset that was never punished. The tradeable object is the conjunction, which is why the output is usually short.

1 — Depth

Log drawdown from the highest close of the last five years. This is long-horizon reversal, measured over the three-to-five-year window where losers eventually turn, not the twelve months where they keep losing.

2 — Duration

Months since that high. Cheap, and it does more work than it looks like: it removes the entire population of fast crashes that have not finished crashing, and freshly unwound parabolas along with them.

3 — No new lows

Trading days since the last running 252-day low, bounded on both sides. The lower bound is what separates this from a falling knife. The upper bound is fixed at 504 days and requires that a low actually happened within the past two years, so an asset that drifted quietly sideways for a decade without ever being punished does not qualify. This gate also hands you the invalidation level, which is most of its practical value.

4 — Curvature

The 100-day moving average rising over the trailing 60 sessions, and price above that average. The slope on its own has an artifact: the average moves by the new bar minus the dropped bar, so a large down day rolling out of the window can turn it positive with no new information in current price. Price above the average cannot be produced by old data leaving the sample, which is what closes the hole.

This is the slowest gate to confirm and will often fire months after gate 5, because the base has to build before an average can turn. That is the case for switching it off, treating gates 1, 2, 3 and 5 as the watchlist, and using the turn as the trigger.

5 — Volatility contraction

Realised volatility over 20 sessions, ranked against its own five-year distribution, required to be low now and to have been high within the last 18 months. Compression on its own picks up assets that have simply always been quiet. Compression following expansion is what a base looks like, and it is the condition that makes the eventual move violent.

Asymmetry is not a gate

The gates decide whether the setup exists. Whether you want it is separate and continuous. Each row reports the distance down to the base low, the distance up to the prior high, and the ratio between them. A name that cleared depth by 60% but sits far above its invalidation level is a worse holding than one that barely cleared at 36% and sits just above it.

Reading the funnel

The strip in the header is the count surviving each gate. If one gate eliminates almost everything and the rest eliminate nothing, the screen is that gate wearing a costume. If the last number reads two, that is the screen telling you the setup is absent right now — not handing you a shortlist. Loosening thresholds until something appears is how a screen becomes a random number generator.

Ratios

The same five gates run on each ratio series, with drawdown measured in log space so a fall from 1.0 to 0.5 and one from 0.5 to 0.25 are the same event. Nothing here assumes a ratio mean-reverts — most macro ratios are regime-switching with very long regimes, and a z-score against a fixed window would have had you long commodities in 1998 and short in 2008, both wrong at the extreme. What the cascade remains exposed to is regime persistence: a ratio can clear every gate and then resume a fifteen-year decline.

What it does not do

It cannot tell you the base rate. Of everything that clears a deep drawdown plus six months without a new low, some meaningful fraction goes on to make a new low anyway, and only an event study over decades will say what that fraction is. It has no non-price anchor, so an asset in genuine terminal decline can clear every gate. And events cluster: one bad October fires forty of these at once, which is one observation and not forty. This is a research tool, not investment advice.

Fixed settings

Held constant so the panel stays small: 5-year window for the reference high; 252-day window for new lows with a 504-day cap; 100-day average with a 60-day slope window; 20-day volatility ranked over five years, with the prior expansion looked for over 18 months above the 70th percentile.
